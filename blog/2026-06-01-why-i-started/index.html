Why I Started This

I got tired of searching for a local business and finding only a Facebook page from 2019. So I did something about it.

campaignorigin

I've lost count of the number of times I've searched for a local business — a café, a plumber, a music teacher — and found nothing but a Facebook page last updated two years ago.

No opening hours. No address. No way to know if they're still trading. Just a post from 2022 and a phone number buried in the About section.

It's a small frustration but it's a persistent one. And after the tenth or eleventh time it happened I decided to do something about it.

What The Web Is Yours Is

Simple offer. We build a free one-page website for small businesses that only exist on social media. We buy the domain. We host it free for the first year. After that it costs about €15 a year to keep the domain.

One page. Their name. What they do. Where they are. How to contact them. Done.

Why One Page

Because one page is enough. Most small local businesses don't need a blog, an online shop, or a booking system. They need people to be able to find them, know they're open, and know how to get in touch.

One page does that. And one page is something a volunteer web developer can build in an afternoon and hand over with a clear conscience.

Why Free

Because the barrier to having a website shouldn't be money. It should be — do you want one? That's the only question.

The domain costs about €15. We cover that. Hosting on Cloudflare Pages is genuinely free. So the whole thing costs us about €15 per business and a few hours of time.

That felt like a reasonable thing to give.

Starting Small

We're starting with one business a month. In the west of Ireland, where I live. One business, one page, one month of someone's time.

If other web developers want to join us and do the same in their area — brilliant. If not, we'll keep going anyway.

Still finding our way. You can too.

Part of The Web Is Yours — a grassroots campaign giving small businesses a home on the web they actually own.
